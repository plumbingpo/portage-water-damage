Kitchen Water Damage in Portage

No room has as many water connections as the kitchen, with its faucet, drain, disposal, dishwasher and refrigerator line. Almost every one sits out of view, behind an appliance or inside a cabinet. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. We help Portage homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

In a wet kitchen, the cabinets are where the real expense lies. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

What It Looks Like

  • Downstairs, a brownish mark on the ceiling beneath the kitchen
  • Behind the dishwasher or sink, a wall with staining or blistered paint
  • Opening the sink cabinet releases a stale, damp odor
  • The bottom edges of cabinet side panels swelling or flaking apart
  • Sounds of dripping or hissing with all taps closed, or an unexplained rise in the water bill
  • Along the cabinet line or beside the fridge, vinyl that feels soft or looks stained
Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Toe Kick Space

Base cabinets sit over a gap that is boxed in all around. Water collects there, stays for weeks, and grows mold that nobody sees until the cabinets are pulled.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

The wall at the back of the sink and dishwasher holds drain and supply pipes. When they leak, framing and drywall get wet behind the cabinets, and mold spreads where no one can see it.

The Dishwasher Cavity

Slowly dripping supply or drain fittings soak the cabinet sides beside the unit and the subfloor below. None of it is visible behind the appliance until the flooring starts to change.

Drips at the Sink Drain

Disposal seals and drain fittings that are loose leak only when water is going down, which makes them easy to overlook. Food waste rides along in that water, so the sink cabinet turns smelly and moldy quickly.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Shut Off the Leak

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Map the Moisture

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry in Place

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. An antimicrobial is applied to damp cavities while air movers and dehumidifiers work over several days.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

We carefully remove any flooring, subfloor or cabinet section that cannot be saved. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first hour

Water Goes Underneath

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Days 3 through 7

Flooring Starts to Move

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

Past one week

Removal Becomes Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.
