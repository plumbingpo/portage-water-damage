Ceiling Water Damage Repair in Portage

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. Long before you see discoloration, the insulation is wet, moisture has followed the framing and a puddle sits on the hidden side of the panel. The leak may be a bathroom on the floor above, a pipe in the ceiling, an air conditioner drain line or the roof. We trace it to the source before anything gets patched.

Covering the mark with paint is not a repair. If the cavity above is still wet, the mark comes back and mold can start on the paper backing within 24 to 48 hours. Our crew removes no more ceiling than necessary, takes out soaked insulation, dries the framing and the drywall that stays, and finishes with a patch, texture and paint that match. Sagging changes the picture. Do not stand beneath it, and get help on the way.

What It Looks Like

  • Drywall that sags, bows or feels soft when pressed gently
  • Stains or streaks running down the wall just below the ceiling line
  • Popcorn or knockdown texture that flakes, drops or turns darker
  • Dark spots or speckled growth forming on the ceiling surface
  • One room that smells damp even though nothing looks wet
  • Yellow or brown rings that grow after rain or after someone showers
If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

Moisture follows the joists and the insulation drinks it in. Because the panel soaks from its top side, nothing may look wrong from underneath.

Hours 24 to 48

Marks Start Showing

A stain forms and paint starts to blister. The paper layer becomes a place mold is able to grow, and heavy wet insulation bears down from above.

Days 3 through 7

Sagging Sets In

The panel turns soft and starts slipping off its fasteners. The ceiling bows between joists, seams open up and a musty odor reaches the room.

Past one week

Risk of Collapse

Saturated pieces may drop and carry soaked insulation down too. Mold is established in the cavity and the repair now covers a much larger area.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Source

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team follows the water to its origin and inspects the attic or room overhead. Until the water stops coming, fixing the ceiling would not last.

2

Make It Safe

Furnishings are protected or relocated, and electricity to affected lights is turned off. Where water is trapped overhead, it gets drained under control into containers.

3

Remove and Dry Out

We take down the ruined drywall and the soaked insulation. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the joists and surrounding ceiling, and we apply antimicrobial where the water was not clean.

4

Rebuild and Paint

Once moisture readings are normal we install new insulation and drywall, tape and finish the seams, match the existing texture and prime with a stain blocker before painting.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Sudden Collapse

Wet drywall is far heavier than dry and loses its grip on the fasteners. Whatever sits under a swollen area is at risk if that section comes down.

The Leak Is Somewhere Else

The wet spot is often not below the leak, since water rides pipes, ducts and joists before it falls. Cutting in just under the mark may leave damp areas elsewhere untouched.

Wet Lights and Wiring

Recessed lights, fans and junction boxes sit right in the wet zone. Turn off that room's breaker, because a wet fixture may short out.

What the Upstairs Is Hiding

A wet ceiling usually means a wet subfloor or attic deck directly over it. Leaving it out of the drying plan lets dampness linger, so both levels get inspected.
