Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Portage: What to Know

The tank on a water heater stores 40 to 80 gallons, yet a leak can release far more than that. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

In Portage, we start with water extraction and then inspect everything around the heater. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. When the heater is in the garage, items kept on the slab get wet and so does the wall adjoining the house. Water from an attic unit soaks through insulation, then through the ceiling. Each of those areas is metered, then dried using dehumidifiers and air movers.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Cut the Power or GasFor an electric heater, switch off its breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to off. A burner or heating element must not stay on once the tank is empty.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Fabric and cardboard absorb water fast, then keep it pressed to the concrete and walls.
Common Causes

What Causes It

  • The drain valve at the bottom of the tank cracking, loosening or failing to shut fully.
  • Water discharged when the pressure and temperature relief valve fails or opens.
  • An overflow pan that cannot drain, because its line is clogged or the pan itself has rusted.
  • A worn anode rod that no longer protects the tank lining from rust.
  • An aging steel tank whose bottom is eaten through by corrosion and sediment.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Heater Closet Drywall

Water wicks up the drywall around an interior heater closet and into the rooms on the other side. The lower wall remains wet on the inside, and the baseboards swell.

Garage Wall Base

The wall between the garage and the house takes water into its bottom plate and drywall from the slab, and from there it reaches the floors of the living space.

Attic and Ceilings

When an attic heater leaks, water passes through the insulation and lands on the ceiling underneath. Soaked insulation loses its insulating value and gets heavy enough to collapse the drywall ceiling.

Stand and Subfloor Damage

Under the tank, the wood stand or the subfloor absorbs the largest share of the leak. Rot can weaken it, a real concern once a full new tank sits on top.

Our Process

How We Handle Water Heater Leak Cleanup

1

Extract the Water

We remove standing water from the floor, closet or garage and, for attic units, from the overflow pan and ceiling cavity, so the surrounding materials stop absorbing it.

2

Check Around the Heater

We go over the wall bases, the platform, any ceiling underneath and the adjoining rooms with thermal imaging and moisture meters, which shows how far the wet area extends.

3

Take Out What Will Not Dry

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. For an attic leak, the wet insulation is cleared out, which lets us dry the drywall and ceiling joists beneath.

4

Dry and Monitor

We keep the drying equipment going for the usual 3 to 5 days needed to dry the space. We record moisture readings and tell you when the space is ready for the new heater.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Until the claim is closed, hold on to the old heater. Photograph it along with the damage, and review your policy.
