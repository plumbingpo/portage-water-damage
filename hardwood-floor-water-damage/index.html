Hardwood Floor Water Damage: What Portage Homeowners Should Know

It does not take long for water to change a wood floor. Boards take on moisture from below and from the edges, swell against each other and start to cup, with the edges rising higher than the center. That does not mean the floor is lost. Boards of solid wood often return to flat if they are dried during those early days. Success depends on a slow, even drying rate that draws water from the flooring and from the subfloor beneath it.

Drying mats are how our team handles wet wood floors. A vacuum unit attached to the sealed mats lifts water out by way of the seams and the wood itself, and dehumidifiers hold the room air at a dry level. Compared with carpet or drywall it is slower, commonly a week or more. Sanding has to wait until the wood is fully dry. Sand a cupped floor too soon and it will crown later.

What It Looks Like

  • Flooring that gives under your weight in the area that got wet
  • Fasteners working loose, heard as squeaks where the floor was soaked
  • Crowning, meaning the middle of each board bulges above its edges
  • Board edges sitting higher than the centers, called cupping
  • Blackish or gray discoloration at board joints and fasteners
  • Puffy baseboards and casings next to the affected boards

What Happens After You Call

1

Take Baseline Readings

Readings are taken in the boards and the subfloor, and on dry flooring close by as a reference. That tells us how deep the water went and whether saving the floor is realistic.

2

Remove Surface Water

We extract any standing water immediately and clear furniture and rugs from the wet section. Cutting off the water entering the joints early keeps swelling down.

3

Install the Mat System

Floor mats are taped down and connected to a vacuum system that pulls moisture up through the boards. The room is managed with dehumidifiers so drying stays even and the boards do not crack.

4

Track Progress and Report

Every day or two we take new readings, continuing until the subfloor and boards match the dry reference spots. At that point you hear from us if it needs nothing, some replacement boards, or a refinish.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Seepage Below the Boards

Seams let the water through, and it fans out in the layer between flooring and subfloor. You can towel off the top and still have a saturated underside.

24 to 48 hours

Cupping Starts

Boards swell and the edges rise. Finish may turn cloudy. The bare underside of the boards and the subfloor are where mold can start.

Day 3 to 7

Boards Start Lifting

Pressure grows through the whole floor as the wood expands. With fasteners losing their hold and boards compressing at the edges, some areas can lift away from the subfloor.

Past one week

Permanent Damage

Expect a good chance of mold below, dark staining and boards that have buckled. Drying is usually no longer enough, and the bulk of the wet flooring has to be replaced.

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Stains From Nails and Staples

Nails and staples that stay wet begin to rust and lose holding power. The rust also bleeds into the wood as dark stains that sanding may not remove.

Damage Beyond the Room

Moisture beneath flooring does not stay put. Next door rooms, the area under cabinets and walls, and the ceiling below are all places it can end up.

Refinishing Before It Is Dry

Leveling a damp, cupped floor with a sander takes wood off the lifted edges. Once drying is complete, the middles sit higher and you have a crowned floor.

Growth Beneath the Floor

Between the boards and the subfloor there is only darkness and still air. Mold in that layer cannot be seen from the room, and a musty odor is usually the first sign.
